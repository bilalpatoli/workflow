# **1\. Title**

SOP: How BPO Sales Development Representatives Find, Qualify, and Book SMB Advertisers for OpenAI’s Advertising Platform

# **2\. Goal**

After completing this training, the BPO Sales Development Representative should be able to identify SMB advertisers that may be a fit for OpenAI’s advertising platform, research their business, understand their current advertising activity, qualify their budget and intent, explain the platform clearly, avoid unsupported claims, book qualified meetings with the internal OpenAI sales team, and log every step correctly in CRM.

The BDR is not expected to close deals, negotiate pricing, promise performance, or answer advanced platform questions. Their job is to create qualified sales conversations.

A trained rep should be able to answer this question: Is this business likely to spend money on advertising, do they have a clear customer acquisition goal, are they open to testing a new discovery channel, and should an AE speak with them?

# **3\. Role Overview**

The BPO Sales Development Representative is responsible for outbound prospecting and qualification.

The role includes researching SMB accounts, finding decision-makers, identifying advertising signals, reaching out through email, phone, LinkedIn, and approved channels, asking discovery questions, qualifying fit, booking meetings, and logging complete notes in CRM.

The role does not include closing deals, setting pricing, claiming guaranteed results, promising ad placements, making targeting claims without approved materials, answering technical implementation questions beyond basic platform positioning, or presenting OpenAI Ads as a replacement for Google, Meta, TikTok, SEO, or agencies.

The BDR should act like a sharp front-line sales operator. Not a script reader. Not a spammer. Not a product expert pretending to know everything. Their job is to be clear, curious, disciplined, and accurate.

# **4\. Product Overview**

OpenAI’s advertising platform should be explained in simple SMB language.

OpenAI’s advertising platform is a new way for businesses to reach potential customers while they are researching products, services, vendors, and decisions inside an AI assistant experience.

For example, a person might be comparing local dental options, researching personal injury lawyers, looking for a home services provider, planning a trip, evaluating software, comparing medspas, or researching a purchase. The advertising platform gives businesses a potential way to appear in relevant advertising experiences while users are actively exploring those topics.

The key idea is simple: people are no longer only searching through traditional search engines or scrolling on social media. They are also asking AI assistants for help understanding their options, comparing providers, and making decisions. OpenAI’s advertising platform gives SMB advertisers a new channel to test for discovery and customer acquisition.

Simple explanation for SMBs: OpenAI Ads is a new discovery channel. It helps businesses explore ways to reach people while they research decisions inside AI assistant experiences. It is not meant to replace Google or Meta. It is another channel to test alongside what already works.

BDRs should keep the explanation simple. Do not get technical unless official sales materials provide technical language.

# **5\. Target Customer Profile**

The best-fit customer is a small or midsize business that already spends money to acquire customers and has a clear reason to test a new advertising channel.

Strong target industries include law firms, dental offices, medspas, home services companies, auto repair shops, real estate brokerages, travel and hospitality companies, eCommerce brands, B2B service companies, and local service businesses with clear conversion goals.

Best-fit businesses usually have a real website, a clear service or product, a defined customer type, an existing marketing budget, active ads or evidence of recent ads, a lead form or booking flow, reviews or social proof, and a decision-maker who cares about growth.

The BDR should prioritize businesses where one new customer has meaningful value.

Examples include a personal injury law firm, cosmetic dentist, medspa, roofing company, HVAC company, luxury travel agency, B2B consultant, or eCommerce brand with repeat purchasing potential.

Weak-fit examples include a brand-new business with no website, no budget, no offer, no conversion path, no reviews, and no ability to handle inbound interest.

# **6\. Best-Fit Business Signals**

A BDR should look for these signals before adding a prospect to outreach.

## **Website Signals**

* The business has an active website.  
* The website clearly explains what they sell.  
* The website has a lead form, booking button, phone number, checkout flow, consultation CTA, quote form, or appointment scheduler.  
* The site looks maintained and not abandoned.  
* The business has clear service pages, pricing pages, product pages, or offer pages.

## **Advertising Signals**

* The business appears to be running Google Ads, Meta Ads, TikTok Ads, LinkedIn Ads, Yelp Ads, Local Services Ads, or other paid campaigns.  
* The business has landing pages built for conversion.  
* The business has tracking pixels, analytics, or retargeting tags.  
* The business is promoting offers, seasonal campaigns, consultations, demos, bookings, or lead magnets.  
* The business has active social content that points to campaigns or promotions.

## **Business Maturity Signals**

* The business has at least 20 to 50 public reviews, depending on industry.  
* The business has multiple locations or serves a defined market.  
* The business has a visible owner, founder, partner, marketing manager, practice manager, office manager, growth leader, or sales leader.  
* The business has clear operating capacity to handle more leads.  
* The business has a strong customer lifetime value.

## **Conversion Goal Signals**

* The business likely values calls, forms, bookings, demos, purchases, consultations, appointments, quote requests, or store visits.  
* The BDR should be able to state the likely conversion goal in one sentence before outreach.  
* Example: Atlas Injury Lawyers likely wants more qualified injury consultations from people actively researching legal options after an accident.

# **7\. Disqualification Criteria**

Do not escalate a prospect if the business shows clear signs it is not ready.

## **Business Disqualifiers**

* No website.  
* Website is broken, outdated, or has no clear CTA.  
* No clear product or service.  
* No clear geographic market or customer type.  
* No reviews or credibility signals.  
* No way to contact or book.  
* No evidence the business can handle more leads.  
* The business appears inactive.

## **Budget and Intent Disqualifiers**

* They do not spend on advertising and have no interest in testing.  
* They expect free leads.  
* They want guaranteed customers before spending.  
* They only want organic ChatGPT recommendations.  
* They ask for guaranteed ranking, guaranteed placement, or guaranteed revenue.  
* They are not willing to meet with a sales rep.

## **Compliance and Category Disqualifiers**

* The business is in a restricted or high-risk category based on official ad policy.  
* Examples may include illegal products, adult sexual services, weapons, counterfeit goods, misleading financial products, deceptive healthcare claims, or other categories prohibited by official OpenAI advertising policy.  
* If unsure, the BDR should mark the account as Policy Review Needed instead of pitching aggressively.

## **Operational Disqualifiers**

* The business cannot answer inbound calls.  
* The business has no sales process.  
* The business cannot track leads.  
* The business has no booking process.  
* The business is already overwhelmed and cannot handle new demand.  
* The business wants leads but has no plan to respond quickly.

# **8\. Research Workflow**

The rep must research every account before outreach. No generic blasting.

## **Step 1: Confirm Basic Business Fit**

* Find the company website.  
* Confirm industry.  
* Confirm location or service area.  
* Confirm business size if possible.  
* Confirm the business is active.  
* Confirm the business serves customers who make buying decisions online.

## **Step 2: Identify the Conversion Goal**

* Ask: What does this business probably want more of?  
* Examples: more consultations, appointments, quote requests, phone calls, online orders, demos, bookings, store visits, or form fills.  
* Log this as Likely Conversion Goal.

## **Step 3: Review Website Quality**

* Does the homepage explain the offer quickly?  
* Is there a CTA above the fold?  
* Is there a form or booking link?  
* Is the phone number visible?  
* Are service pages clear?  
* Is the site mobile-friendly enough to convert?  
* Does the site show trust signals like reviews, logos, case results, before and afters, certifications, awards, or testimonials?

## **Step 4: Check Advertising Activity**

* Look for signs of active paid acquisition, including Google sponsored results, Meta Ad Library, landing pages, UTM links, retargeting pixels, promoted social posts, Yelp or directory sponsorship, Local Services Ads, affiliate activity, influencer activity, and email list growth offers.  
* If no direct ad signal is found, mark Ad Activity Unknown, not No Ads.

## **Step 5: Review Social and Content**

* Check LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, Google Business Profile, and industry directories.  
* Look for recent posts, promotions, new location announcements, hiring posts, seasonal offers, customer education, reviews, before and after content, case results, events, and expansion signals.

## **Step 6: Identify the Decision-Maker**

* Priority order: owner or founder, managing partner, practice owner, marketing director, growth manager, office manager, practice manager, general manager, or operations manager.  
* For eCommerce or B2B, prioritize founder, CEO, Head of Growth, Marketing Director, Demand Gen Manager, VP Sales, or Revenue Leader.

## **Step 7: Write a One-Line Account Insight**

* Before outreach, the rep must write one account insight.  
* Format: I noticed \[specific business signal\], which suggests \[likely growth opportunity\].  
* Example: I noticed you are running dedicated landing pages for dental implants, which suggests you are already investing in high-intent patient acquisition.

## **Step 8: Choose the Outreach Angle**

* Pick one angle only.  
* Examples: new discovery channel, more efficient customer acquisition testing, reaching people during research moments, adding OpenAI Ads alongside current Google and Meta campaigns, or testing intent from AI-assisted decision-making.  
* Do not try to sell every benefit in one message.

The rep should use a structured account prioritization system.

## **Prospect Sources**

* CRM reactivation lists.  
* Inbound free trial or waitlist signups if available.

## **Prioritization Tiers**

### **Tier 1: Highest Priority**

Businesses that already advertise, have clear conversion goals, strong reviews, good website, visible decision-maker, and high customer value. These should get deeper personalization and may receive a Loom or manual email.

### **Tier 2: Good Priority**

Businesses with strong business fit but unclear ad spend. These should get standard personalized outreach.

### **Tier 3: Low Priority**

Businesses with some fit but weak website, unclear budget, limited reviews, or no visible growth signal. These may be added to lower-touch nurture only if approved.

### **Do Not Prospect**

Businesses with no website, no clear offer, no active presence, restricted categories, or obvious inability to convert leads.

# **10\. Outreach Requirements**

Strong outreach must be specific, short, accurate, and tied to a business outcome. The cadence should use varied touches across email, phone, LinkedIn, and optional SMS where appropriate.

## **Every First-Touch Email Must Include**

* A relevant subject line.  
* A specific reason for reaching out.  
* One researched observation.  
* A simple explanation of OpenAI Ads.  
* A business-relevant outcome.  
* A low-friction CTA.  
* No hype.  
* No unsupported claims.  
* No fake familiarity.  
* No giant paragraphs.

## **Strong Email Structure**

* Line 1: Why you are reaching out.  
* Line 2: Specific observation.  
* Line 3: Simple positioning.  
* Line 4: Soft CTA.  
* Example: I noticed \[Company\] is already investing in \[channel or offer\]. OpenAI is opening a new advertising channel for businesses to reach people while they research decisions inside AI assistant experiences. Thought it may be worth exploring as an additional test channel alongside what you already run. Open to a quick intro next week?

## **LinkedIn Message Requirements**

* Keep it short.  
* Mention the business or role.  
* Do not pitch hard in the connection request.  
* Use LinkedIn to start a conversation, not dump a product description.

## **Call Opener Requirements**

* Ask for permission.  
* Give context.  
* Mention a specific reason for calling.  
* Explain value in one sentence.  
* Ask one qualifying question.

Example: Hi Sarah, this is Alex with OpenAI. I know I am catching you cold. I noticed your medspa is already promoting consultations, and I am reaching out because OpenAI is speaking with select SMB advertisers about a new discovery channel inside AI assistant experiences. Are you currently testing new ways to bring in booked consults, or mostly focused on Google and Meta right now?

# **11\. Approved Positioning**

## **Simple Positioning**

OpenAI Ads is a new discovery channel for businesses that want to reach people while they are researching decisions inside an AI assistant experience.

## **Channel Positioning**

It is not a replacement for Google, Meta, or SEO. It is another channel to test alongside the channels already driving customer acquisition.

## **Intent Positioning**

The idea is to reach potential customers during research moments, when they are comparing options, learning about solutions, or deciding what provider to contact.

## **SMB Positioning**

For SMBs, this may be useful if you already know what a qualified lead or booking is worth and you want to test a new source of demand.

## **Qualification Positioning**

We are mostly speaking with businesses that already spend on advertising, have a clear conversion goal, and want to evaluate whether this channel makes sense for them.

## **Meeting Positioning**

The next step is not a purchase commitment. It is a qualified conversation with the OpenAI sales team to understand your goals, current ad mix, and whether there is a fit.

# **12\. Claims Reps Must Avoid**

This section is non-negotiable.

## **BDRs must never say:**

* Ads influence organic ChatGPT answers.  
* The business will be recommended by ChatGPT.  
* OpenAI Ads guarantees leads, bookings, cases, patients, customers, revenue, ROAS, or ROI.  
* OpenAI Ads replaces Google, Meta, TikTok, SEO, agencies, or existing marketing.  
* Every business is a fit.  
* The business will appear in a specific placement unless official materials confirm that placement.  
* The business can target any exact keyword, audience, user type, conversation, location, or category unless approved sales materials say so.  
* The platform has specific pricing unless approved pricing materials are provided.  
* The platform has specific performance benchmarks unless approved benchmarks are provided.  
* The platform can provide preferential treatment inside organic AI responses.  
* The platform can manipulate AI assistant recommendations.  
* The advertiser can pay to be the answer.  
* The business will be shown ahead of competitors.  
* The ad will only show to people ready to buy.  
* The platform can access private user conversations for targeting unless official policy says so.  
* The platform is cheaper than Google or Meta.  
* The platform works better than Google or Meta.  
* The platform is risk-free.  
* The platform does not require tracking, testing, or optimization.

## **Safe Correction Language**

If a prospect asks, Will this make ChatGPT recommend us?

The rep should say: No, that is not how we should think about it. Ads are separate from organic assistant responses. The opportunity is to test paid advertising experiences where available, not to influence organic answers.

If a prospect asks, Can you guarantee leads?

The rep should say: No, we cannot guarantee results. The goal of the call is to understand whether your business is a good fit to test the channel based on your goals, budget, offer, and current marketing.

# **13\. Discovery Questions**

The rep should ask clear questions that qualify fit without turning the call into an interrogation.

## **Business Fit**

* What does your business mainly sell?  
* Who is your ideal customer?  
* Are you focused on local, regional, national, or online customers?  
* What services, products, or offers are you most focused on growing right now?  
* What makes a customer valuable for you?

## **Current Advertising**

* Are you currently running paid ads?  
* Which channels are you using today?  
* What is working best right now?  
* What is not working?  
* Are you managing ads in-house or with an agency?

## **Conversion Goals**

* What would you want more of?  
* Calls, forms, bookings, consultations, purchases, demos, quote requests, or store visits?  
* What happens after a lead comes in?  
* Who follows up?  
* How fast does your team respond?  
* Are calls tracked?  
* Are form fills tracked?  
* Are booked appointments tracked?

## **Budget**

* Roughly how much are you spending per month on customer acquisition?  
* Are you testing new channels this quarter?  
* Do you already have budget allocated for new experiments?  
* What budget range would make sense for testing a new channel if the fit is there?

## **Urgency**

* Why are you looking at new channels now?  
* Are you trying to lower cost per acquisition?  
* Scale a specific service line?  
* Fill a new location?  
* Improve lead quality?  
* Diversify away from one channel?  
* Launch a new offer?

## **Decision Process**

* Are you the person who evaluates new advertising channels?  
* Who else would need to be involved?  
* If the OpenAI team showed you a strong fit, what would the next step usually look like internally?

## **Compliance and Readiness**

* Are there any restrictions in your industry around advertising claims?  
* Do you have approved landing pages or ad copy guidelines?  
* Are there services or claims you avoid promoting?  
* Do you have tracking in place today?

# **14\. Qualification Checklist**

A lead should only be escalated if it passes the checklist.

## **Required Pass Criteria**

* The business is active.  
* The business has a website.  
* The business has a clear product or service.  
* The business has a clear customer acquisition goal.  
* The business is in an eligible category.  
* The business already spends on advertising or has stated intent to test paid acquisition.  
* The business has a decision-maker identified.  
* The business has a conversion path.  
* The business can explain what a qualified lead, booking, purchase, or customer means.  
* The business is open to a meeting with OpenAI sales.

## **Strong Fit Criteria**

* Existing monthly ad spend.  
* High-value customer.  
* Multi-location or growth-oriented business.  
* Active landing pages or campaign pages.  
* Good reviews.  
* Strong offer.  
* Tracking in place.  
* Sales team or intake team exists.  
* Clear budget range.  
* Timeline within 90 days.  
* Decision-maker attending the meeting.

## **Fail Criteria**

* No website.  
* No budget.  
* No interest.  
* Unclear offer.  
* Restricted category.  
* Wants guaranteed results.  
* Wants to pay to influence organic ChatGPT responses.  
* Cannot handle leads.  
* No decision-maker.  
* Refuses to share basic marketing context.  
* No conversion goal.

## **Lead Status Recommendation**

* Qualified, escalate to AE.  
* Nurture, not ready now.  
* Disqualified, no fit.  
* Policy review needed.  
* Research incomplete.

# **16\. AE Escalation Criteria**

Escalate to an AE when the prospect meets the minimum fit threshold and there is a real reason for a sales conversation.

## **Escalate When**

* The business has active advertising or stated intent to test paid ads.  
* The business has a clear conversion goal.  
* The business is in an approved industry.  
* The business has budget or likely budget.  
* A decision-maker or strong influencer is engaged.  
* The prospect understands this is a test channel, not a guarantee.  
* The prospect is open to discussing fit with OpenAI sales.  
* The BDR has logged complete CRM notes.

## **Do Not Escalate When**

* The prospect only wants pricing but has shared no business context.  
* The prospect asks for guaranteed results.  
* The prospect wants to influence organic ChatGPT answers.  
* The prospect is in a restricted category.  
* The prospect has no budget.  
* The prospect is not the right contact and refuses to identify the decision-maker.  
* The BDR has not completed research.

## **AE Handoff Note**

* Why the account is a fit.  
* What the business sells.  
* What they want more of.  
* Current ad channels.  
* Estimated spend.  
* Decision-maker status.  
* Main objection or concern.  
* Any compliance risk.  
* What the BDR promised.

The BDR should never write vague notes like: Good call, interested. That is not enough.

# **17\. Quality Standards**

Good BDR execution looks like this:

* The rep researches before outreach.  
* The rep contacts the right person.  
* The rep writes a specific first message.  
* The rep explains the platform simply.  
* The rep does not overpromise.  
* The rep asks business questions before pitching too much.  
* The rep qualifies budget, authority, need, timing, and fit.  
* The rep books meetings only when there is a real reason.  
* The rep logs complete notes.  
* The rep follows the approved cadence.  
* The rep honors opt-outs.  
* The rep uses judgment.  
* The rep sounds like a real person, not an automated script.

A strong rep should be able to say: I reached out because this business is already spending on acquisition, has a clear conversion path, and likely cares about reaching people during research moments. I did not promise performance. I booked the meeting because they have budget, intent, and decision-maker involvement.

| Subject: Get customers from ChatGPT Hi John, OpenAI is launching ads inside ChatGPT and your law firm can now pay to show up when people ask for personal injury lawyers. This will help you get recommended by ChatGPT instead of relying on Google Ads. We can guarantee better leads because people using ChatGPT are already looking for answers. This could replace a lot of your current ad spend and get you more signed cases fast. Do you want to book a call this week? Thanks, Alex |
| :---- |

### **Why This Is Bad**

* It claims the business can be recommended by ChatGPT.  
* It implies ads affect organic AI answers.  
* It guarantees better leads.  
* It suggests the platform replaces Google Ads.  
* It makes unsupported performance claims.  
* It has no real personalization.  
* It skips qualification.  
* It is risky and not approved.

## **20\. Example Good Outreach**

| Subject: Question about new client acquisition for Morgan Injury Law Hi Sarah, I noticed Morgan Injury Law is already investing in search visibility around injury consultations in Dallas, so I figured you may be open to testing new acquisition channels if the fit is right. OpenAI is speaking with SMB advertisers about a new discovery channel where businesses can reach people while they are researching products, services, vendors, and decisions inside an AI assistant experience. Not a replacement for Google or Meta, and not a guarantee of results. More of a potential test channel for businesses that already know what a qualified lead is worth. Would it be worth a quick intro with the OpenAI team to see if this could fit your current marketing mix? Best, Alex |
| :---- |

### **Why This Is Good**

* It references a specific business signal.  
* It explains the platform simply.  
* It avoids overpromising.  
* It makes clear this is not a Google or Meta replacement.  
* It positions the meeting as fit exploration.  
* It is short and easy to respond to.

# **21\. QA Rubric**

Use this 100-point rubric to grade outreach, discovery calls, and CRM notes.

## **A. Account Selection and Research: 15 Points**

* 15 points: Strong-fit account, clear ad activity or intent, complete research, correct decision-maker, clear conversion goal.  
* 10 points: Mostly good fit, some research gaps, likely decision-maker identified.  
* 5 points: Weak research, unclear ad activity, vague conversion goal.  
* 0 points: Bad-fit account, no website, no clear offer, restricted category, or no research.

## **B. Personalization: 10 Points**

* 10 points: Outreach includes a specific, accurate insight tied to the business.  
* 7 points: Some personalization but could apply to many similar businesses.  
* 3 points: Generic industry mention only.  
* 0 points: No personalization or fake personalization.

## **C. Product Accuracy: 15 Points**

* 15 points: Explains platform clearly as a discovery channel and stays within approved claims.  
* 10 points: Mostly accurate but slightly vague.  
* 5 points: Confusing explanation or too much unsupported detail.  
* 0 points: Claims ads influence organic answers, guarantees results, or invents product capabilities.

## **D. Outreach Quality: 10 Points**

* 10 points: Short, clear, business-focused, strong CTA.  
* 7 points: Clear but too long or slightly generic.  
* 3 points: Too vague, too pitchy, or unclear CTA.  
* 0 points: Spammy, misleading, sloppy, or non-compliant.

## **E. Discovery Quality: 15 Points**

* 15 points: Asks strong questions about business, current ads, conversion goals, budget, timing, and decision process.  
* 10 points: Covers most qualification areas.  
* 5 points: Asks surface-level questions only.  
* 0 points: Does not qualify and only pitches.

## **F. Objection Handling: 10 Points**

* 10 points: Handles objections calmly, corrects misconceptions, and redirects to fit.  
* 7 points: Handles basic objections but misses deeper issue.  
* 3 points: Defensive, vague, or overly scripted.  
* 0 points: Argues, overpromises, or gives false answers.

## **G. Qualification Judgment: 10 Points**

* 10 points: Correctly determines qualified, nurture, disqualified, or policy review.  
* 7 points: Mostly correct but misses one risk.  
* 3 points: Escalates too early or fails to escalate a good lead.  
* 0 points: Books bad-fit leads or ignores disqualification criteria.

## **H. CRM Completeness: 10 Points**

* 10 points: All required fields complete, useful AE summary, clear next step.  
* 7 points: Mostly complete but missing minor details.  
* 3 points: Sparse notes.  
* 0 points: No notes or useless notes like interested.

## **I. Professionalism and Tone: 5 Points**

* 5 points: Clear, respectful, confident, consultative.  
* 3 points: Acceptable but robotic or too casual.  
* 1 point: Sloppy, awkward, or overly aggressive.  
* 0 points: Unprofessional.

## **Scoring Bands**

* 90 to 100: Ready for live outreach.  
* 80 to 89: Ready with light coaching.  
* 70 to 79: Needs targeted retraining before scaling volume.  
* 60 to 69: Needs manager review and roleplay remediation.  
* Below 60: Not cleared for live outreach.

# **22\. Micro-Retraining Triggers**

## **Trigger 1: Weak Personalization**

Detected when: outreach uses generic lines like I came across your website without a specific insight.

Retraining module: How to find one useful business signal in under 3 minutes.

Passing evidence: Rep writes 5 account insights that are specific and accurate.

## **Trigger 2: Unqualified Prospecting**

Detected when: rep adds accounts with no website, no clear offer, no decision-maker, or no conversion path.

Retraining module: How to identify best-fit SMB advertisers.

Passing evidence: Rep reviews 20 accounts and correctly labels qualified, nurture, disqualified, or policy review.

## **Trigger 3: Overpromising**

Detected when: rep says or writes guaranteed leads, guaranteed revenue, better than Google, recommended by ChatGPT, or similar claims.

Retraining module: Approved positioning and forbidden claims.

Passing evidence: Rep rewrites 10 risky claims into compliant language.

## **Trigger 4: Inaccurate Product Claims**

Detected when: rep invents targeting, placement, pricing, or performance details.

Retraining module: How to answer product questions when you do not know.

Passing evidence: Rep correctly handles 10 prospect questions using approved deflection language.

## **Trigger 5: Poor Discovery**

Detected when: rep books meetings without asking about current ads, budget, conversion goal, authority, or timing.

Retraining module: SMB advertiser discovery basics.

Passing evidence: Rep completes a mock discovery call with at least 80 percent rubric score.

## **Trigger 6: Bad CRM Notes**

Detected when: notes are missing, vague, or not useful for AE handoff.

Retraining module: How to write AE-ready CRM notes.

Passing evidence: Rep rewrites 5 bad CRM notes into complete handoff summaries.

## **Trigger 7: Bad Escalation**

Detected when: rep passes leads that are unqualified, restricted, not decision-maker involved, or have no budget.

Retraining module: AE escalation rules.

Passing evidence: Rep reviews 10 sample leads and makes correct escalation decisions.

## **Trigger 8: Failure to Correct Misconceptions**

Detected when: prospect asks whether ads influence ChatGPT answers and rep does not correct them.

Retraining module: Organic answers vs. paid advertising experiences.

Passing evidence: Rep roleplays 5 correction scenarios without overexplaining or making risky claims.

## **Trigger 9: Repetitive Cadence**

Detected when: follow-ups repeat the same CTA or message.

Retraining module: How to vary follow-up angles across a 30-day cadence.

Passing evidence: Rep writes 6 follow-ups using different angles: insight, question, case study, objection, value drop, closeout.

## **Trigger 10: Poor Call Control**

Detected when: rep rambles, fails to ask questions, or does not ask for next step.

Retraining module: Cold call structure and permission-based CTA.

Passing evidence: Rep completes a 2-minute mock cold call and earns at least 4 out of 5 on opening, value prop, and next step.

# Cold Calling SOP

**Cold Calling SOP**

**BPO Sales Development Representative Training**

For SMB Advertiser Prospecting and Qualification

**Purpose: Train outsourced BPO BDRs to call, qualify, and book qualified SMB advertiser meetings for OpenAI sales, without overpromising or making unsupported product claims.**

Version: Cold Calling Only

# **1\. SOP Goal**

This SOP trains BPO Sales Development Representatives to execute cold calls for a hypothetical OpenAI advertising platform focused on small and midsize business advertisers.

After training, a BDR should be able to research an SMB account, call the right decision-maker, explain the advertising opportunity in simple language, qualify current advertising activity, identify budget and intent, handle objections safely, book qualified meetings with the OpenAI sales team, and log complete notes in CRM.

The BDR is not responsible for closing deals, explaining technical product details, discussing pricing, promising performance, or making claims that are not provided in official sales materials.

| Core Job of the BDR |
| :---- |
| Get the right person on the phone. Explain the opportunity clearly. Qualify whether the business already spends on ads or is ready to test paid acquisition. Book a meeting only when there is a real fit. Log the call correctly in CRM. |

# **2\. Role Overview**

The BPO BDR is the front-line qualification role. The rep creates qualified conversations for the internal OpenAI sales team.

* Call SMB advertisers and identify whether they are a fit for a new advertising channel.  
* Ask business questions before pitching too much.  
* Confirm the prospect has a clear offer, conversion goal, and ability to handle leads.  
* Confirm current advertising channels, budget range, decision-maker status, and timeline.  
* Correct misconceptions around AI assistant advertising and organic ChatGPT answers.  
* Book qualified meetings with the internal sales team.  
* Log every attempt, outcome, objection, and next step in CRM.

## **What the BDR Must Not Do**

* Do not close deals.  
* Do not negotiate or quote pricing unless official materials provide it.  
* Do not promise leads, bookings, revenue, ROAS, recommendations, or placement.  
* Do not say ads influence organic ChatGPT answers.  
* Do not say the business will be recommended by ChatGPT.  
* Do not make up targeting, placement, pricing, availability, or performance details.  
* Do not book meetings with businesses that clearly fail qualification standards.

# **3\. Phone-Safe Product Positioning**

The BDR should explain the platform in simple SMB-friendly language. The explanation must be short enough to use on a live cold call.

| Approved Simple Explanation |
| :---- |
| OpenAI Ads is a new discovery channel where businesses may be able to reach people while they are researching products, services, vendors, and buying decisions inside an AI assistant experience. It is not a replacement for Google or Meta. It is another channel to evaluate alongside what already works. |

## **Approved Positioning Lines**

* This is a new discovery channel for businesses that already understand paid customer acquisition.  
* The goal is to explore whether your business could reach people during research and decision-making moments inside an AI assistant experience.  
* This is not about paying to influence organic ChatGPT answers.  
* This is not positioned as a replacement for Google, Meta, TikTok, SEO, or your agency.  
* The sales call is to understand whether the channel is a fit based on your offer, budget, goals, and current marketing mix.

# **4\. Target Customer for Cold Calling**

The BDR should call SMBs that have clear customer acquisition goals and are likely to understand the value of testing paid channels.

* Law firms  
* Dental offices  
* Medspas and cosmetic practices  
* Home services companies  
* Auto repair shops  
* Real estate brokerages  
* Travel and hospitality companies  
* eCommerce brands  
* B2B service companies  
* Local service businesses with calls, bookings, quote requests, purchases, or consultations as the main conversion goal

## **Best-Fit Signals Before Calling**

* Active website with clear offer and CTA.  
* Phone number, booking link, lead form, quote form, checkout, or consultation CTA.  
* Strong reviews or visible social proof.  
* Existing ad activity, landing pages, promoted offers, or growth campaigns.  
* Multi-location presence or clear expansion goal.  
* High-value customer or strong lifetime value.  
* Decision-maker is visible or reachable.  
* Business can handle additional leads or customer demand.

## **Do Not Prioritize**

* Businesses with no website or broken website.  
* Businesses with no clear offer or no conversion path.  
* Businesses in restricted or high-risk categories.  
* Businesses that want to pay to influence organic ChatGPT responses.  
* Businesses that demand guaranteed results before spending.  
* Businesses that cannot answer calls, respond to leads, or process demand.

# **5\. Pre-Call Research Checklist**

Every call should be based on a specific reason. The rep does not need to research for 30 minutes. The rep does need enough context to avoid sounding generic.

1. Find the company website and confirm the business is active.  
2. Identify the likely conversion goal: calls, consultations, appointments, purchases, demos, quote requests, or bookings.  
3. Look for ad activity or campaign signals: sponsored listings, Meta Ad Library, landing pages, pixels, promotions, or active social campaigns.  
4. Review reviews, service pages, booking flow, and location count.  
5. Identify the best decision-maker: owner, founder, managing partner, practice manager, marketing lead, GM, or operations leader.  
6. Write one account insight before dialing.

| Required Account Insight Format |
| :---- |
| I noticed \[specific signal\], which suggests \[likely growth opportunity\]. Example: I noticed your firm has dedicated pages for truck accident and rideshare accident cases, which suggests qualified consultations are probably a high-value growth lever. |

# **6\. Cold Call Outcomes**

The rep must know the desired outcome of the call before dialing.

| Outcome | Definition | Next Step |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Meeting Booked | Prospect is qualified and agrees to meet with internal sales. | Create meeting, log notes, assign AE. |
| Interested, Not Booked | Prospect shows interest but cannot schedule now. | Send approved follow-up and set task. |
| Send Info | Prospect requests information but gives limited qualification. | Ask one qualifying question, send approved info, set follow-up. |
| Nurture | Fit may exist, but timing or budget is not ready. | Set future follow-up. |
| Disqualified | No fit due to offer, budget, category, intent, or lead handling. | Mark disqualified with reason. |
| Wrong Contact | Person is not responsible for advertising decisions. | Ask for correct contact and update CRM. |
| Opt-Out | Prospect asks not to be contacted. | Stop outreach immediately and update CRM. |

# **7\. Core Cold Call Flow**

The call should be clear, controlled, and short. The rep should not turn the opener into a long pitch.

| Step | Goal | What the Rep Should Do |

| 4\. Qualification Question | Start discovery. | Ask if they are testing new ways to drive calls, bookings, consultations, purchases, or leads. |
| 5\. Current Channel Check | Understand marketing maturity. | Ask what they use now: Google, Meta, SEO, TikTok, LinkedIn, Yelp, referrals, agency, or in-house. |

Open the call clearly.

Ask qualification questions.

Handle common objections.

Book a meeting with the right person.

Log clean CRM notes.

Avoid overpromising or making unsupported claims.

## **3\. Role Overview**

The BDR is responsible for starting qualified sales conversations.

The BDR is not responsible for closing the deal, negotiating pricing, giving advanced technical answers, or promising results.

The BDR’s job is to:

Call SMB prospects.

Reach the right person.

Create interest.

Qualify basic fit.

Book a meeting with the sales team.

Document everything clearly.

A strong BDR sounds professional, curious, and direct. They do not sound robotic, pushy, or desperate.

---

## **4\. Target Prospect**

The target prospect is a small or midsize business that may benefit from a new customer acquisition, marketing, software, advertising, or operational solution.

The prospect should have:

An active business.

A clear product or service.

A clear customer type.

A website or online presence.

A way to receive inquiries, calls, bookings, or sales.

A decision-maker or manager who can evaluate new tools, services, or channels.

A possible business reason to take a meeting.

The rep does not need to know the full industry details before calling. They just need enough context to explain why the call may be relevant.

## **5\. Pre-Call Research**

Before calling, the rep should spend a short amount of time reviewing the account.

The rep should identify:

Company name.

Website or online profile.

What the business sells.

Who the likely customer is.

What action the business likely wants more of.

Who the best contact is.

Why the business may be worth calling.

The rep should not over-research before calling. The goal is to have enough context to sound relevant, not spend 20 minutes on one cold call.

## **11\. Qualification Questions**

The rep should ask simple questions to understand fit.

### **Business Need**

“What are you mainly focused on improving right now?”

“Are you looking to grow, save time, improve follow-up, get more customers, or solve a specific operational issue?”

“What is one area of the business you would like to improve over the next few months?”

### **Current Process**

“How are you handling that today?”

“Are you using any tools, vendors, or internal process for that right now?”

“What is working well?”

“What is still frustrating?”

### **Priority**

“Is this a current priority, or more of a future project?”

“Is this something you are actively looking into right now?”

“What would make this worth exploring?”

### **Authority**

“Are you the person who usually evaluates new solutions like this?”

“Would anyone else need to be involved in the conversation?”

### **Budget or Readiness**

“Do you already have budget set aside for new tools or services?”

“Are you open to investing if the business case makes sense?”

“Have you tested anything similar before?”

## **12\. Qualification Checklist**

A prospect is qualified if:

\[ \] The business is active.  
 \[ \] The prospect has a real business problem or growth goal.  
 \[ \] The prospect is open to learning more.  
 \[ \] The prospect is the decision-maker or can involve the decision-maker.  
 \[ \] The prospect has some ability to invest if there is fit.  
 \[ \] The meeting would be useful for the sales team.

A prospect is not qualified if:

\[ \] They have no interest.  
 \[ \] They have no clear need.  
 \[ \] They refuse to answer basic questions.  
 \[ \] They only want guaranteed results.  
 \[ \] They have no ability to invest.  
 \[ \] They are not the right person and will not share who is.  
 \[ \] They ask not to be contacted again.

# **QA Scorecard: General SMB Cold Calling**

## **Purpose**

This QA scorecard is used by managers, team leads, or WorkFlow AI to grade whether a trainee followed the cold calling SOP correctly.

The evaluation should focus on call structure, professionalism, qualification, objection handling, meeting quality, CRM logging, and claim control.

## **Passing Score**

**Pass:** 85 to 100  
 **Needs Coaching:** 70 to 84  
 **Retraining Required:** Below 70

## **Automatic Fail**

The trainee automatically fails if they:

Guarantee results.

Make up pricing.

Misrepresent the product or service.

Ignore an opt-out request.

Book a meeting with no qualification.

Use aggressive, misleading, or unprofessional language.

Fail to log the call.

---

## **QA Category 1: Call Opening and Professionalism**

**10 Points**

### **What QA Should Look For**

The rep introduces themselves clearly.

The rep says the company name.

The rep acknowledges it is a cold call.

The rep keeps the opening short.

The rep sounds calm, respectful, and professional.

The rep does not sound robotic or overly scripted.

### **Full Credit Example**

“Hi \[First Name\], this is \[Rep Name\] with \[Company\]. I know I’m catching you cold, so I’ll be quick.”

### **Scoring**

10: Clear, concise, professional opening  
 7: Good opening but slightly awkward or too long  
 4: Unclear opening or overly scripted  
 0: No proper introduction or unprofessional opening

---

## **QA Category 2: Reason for Calling**

**10 Points**

### **What QA Should Look For**

The rep explains why they are calling.

The rep gives a simple, business-focused reason.

The rep does not launch into a full pitch.

The rep makes the call feel relevant to SMB growth, operations, customer acquisition, or business improvement.

### **Full Credit Example**

“The reason I’m calling is we’re speaking with small and midsize businesses that are looking at new ways to grow, improve customer acquisition, or make operations more efficient.”

### **Scoring**

10: Clear reason for calling  
 7: Mostly clear but slightly generic  
 4: Vague or confusing reason  
 0: No reason given

---

## **QA Category 3: Value Statement**

**10 Points**

### **What QA Should Look For**

The rep gives a simple value statement.

The rep explains the offer in plain language.

The rep does not overcomplicate the call.

The rep does not use jargon.

The rep connects the reason for calling to a business outcome.

### **Scoring**

10: Clear and simple value statement  
 7: Understandable but slightly vague  
 4: Too generic or confusing  
 0: No value statement or misleading explanation

---

## **QA Category 4: Qualification Questions**

**15 Points**

### **What QA Should Look For**

The rep asks questions before trying to book the meeting.

The rep understands whether the prospect has a real need.

The rep asks about current process, goals, priority, authority, and readiness.

### **Required Qualification Areas**

Business need.

Current process.

Priority.

Authority.

Readiness or willingness to invest if there is fit.

### **Strong Questions**

“What are you mainly focused on improving right now?”

“How are you handling that today?”

“Is this a current priority, or more of a future project?”

“Are you the person who usually evaluates new solutions like this?”

“Are you open to investing if the business case makes sense?”

### **Scoring**

15: Covers all major qualification areas  
 10: Covers most areas  
 5: Asks only surface-level questions  
 0: Does not qualify before pitching or booking

---

## **QA Category 5: Listening and Call Control**

**10 Points**

### **What QA Should Look For**

The rep listens to the answer.

The rep does not interrupt.

The rep responds to what the prospect actually says.

The rep keeps the call moving.

The rep does not ramble.

The rep controls the call without being pushy.

### **Scoring**

10: Strong listening and call control  
 7: Mostly good but slightly unfocused  
 4: Interrupts or misses cues  
 0: Talks over prospect or loses control

---

## **QA Category 6: Objection Handling**

**15 Points**

### **What QA Should Look For**

The rep handles objections calmly.

The rep does not argue.

The rep acknowledges the prospect.

The rep gives a clear response.

The rep redirects to a useful next step or qualification question.

### **Common Objections**

“I’m busy.”

“Send me info.”

“We already have something.”

“How much does it cost?”

“Can you guarantee results?”

“Not interested.”

### **Strong Example**

Prospect: “How much does it cost?”

Rep: “Good question. I do not want to give you the wrong answer without understanding fit first. My role is to see if this is worth a conversation. The sales team can walk you through pricing and options based on your business.”

### **Scoring**

15: Handles objection clearly and professionally  
 10: Handles objection but misses a follow-up question  
 5: Weak or awkward response  
 0: Argues, overpromises, or gives inaccurate information

---

## **QA Category 7: Meeting Ask and Next Step**

**10 Points**

### **What QA Should Look For**

The rep only asks for a meeting after basic qualification.

The rep frames the meeting correctly.

The rep makes clear the meeting is not a commitment.

The rep confirms the next step.

The rep confirms date, time, and attendee if booked.

### **Full Credit Example**

“Based on what you shared, this may be worth a quick conversation with our sales team. The call is not a commitment. It is just to understand your business, your goals, and whether this could be a fit. Would Thursday at 2 PM work?”

### **Scoring**

10: Clear, qualified meeting ask  
 7: Meeting ask made but framing could be better  
 4: Weak next step or no confirmed time  
 0: No meeting ask or unqualified meeting booked

---

## **QA Category 8: Claim Control and Accuracy**

**10 Points**

### **What QA Should Look For**

The rep does not guarantee results.

The rep does not make up pricing.

The rep does not overstate the product or service.

The rep does not say the solution works for everyone.

The rep says they do not know when they do not know.

The rep correctly routes detailed questions to the sales team.

### **Scoring**

10: Fully accurate and controlled  
 7: Minor wording issue but no major risk  
 4: Risky language used but corrected  
 0: Unsupported claim, made-up answer, or guarantee

---

## **QA Category 9: CRM Logging**

**10 Points**

### **What QA Should Look For**

The rep logs the call.

The note is useful for the sales team.

The note includes the outcome, need, current process, authority, objection, and next step.

The note is not vague.

### **Required CRM Details**

Company name.

Contact name.

Call outcome.

Business goal or problem.

Current process or current solution.

Decision-maker status.

Interest level.

Objections or questions.

Meeting booked or follow-up date.

Next step.

### **Bad CRM Notes**

“Good call.”

“Interested.”

“Call back.”

“Needs info.”

### **Strong CRM Note**

“Spoke with John at ABC Services. They are focused on improving customer follow-up and bringing in more consistent inquiries. They currently handle most follow-up manually. John is the owner and decision-maker. He asked about pricing, and I explained that the sales team would walk through options after understanding fit. Meeting booked for Thursday at 2 PM.”

### **Scoring**

10: Complete, sales-ready CRM note  
 7: Mostly complete but missing minor details  
 4: Too vague to be useful  
 0: No CRM note

---

Opening and professionalism: 10

# Checklist

# **Trainee Checklist: General SMB Cold Calling**

## **Purpose**

Use this checklist before, during, and after every cold call.

The goal is to make sure the rep is prepared, follows the call structure, qualifies correctly, avoids unsupported claims, books the right meetings, and logs the call properly.

---

# **Pre-Call Checklist**

Before dialing, confirm:

\[ \] I know the company name.  
 \[ \] I know what the business does.  
 \[ \] I know the contact name, if available.  
 \[ \] I know the contact’s likely role.  
 \[ \] I know why this business may be relevant.  
 \[ \] I have one simple reason for calling.  
 \[ \] I have the opening line ready.  
 \[ \] I have qualification questions ready.  
 \[ \] I know what claims to avoid.  
 \[ \] I am ready to log the call immediately after.

---

# **Call Opening Checklist**

The rep must:

\[ \] Introduce themselves clearly.  
 \[ \] Say the company name.  
 \[ \] Acknowledge it is a cold call.  
 \[ \] Keep the opening short.  
 \[ \] Explain the reason for calling.  
 \[ \] Use simple language.  
 \[ \] Ask an opening qualification question.

## **Approved Opener**

“Hi \[First Name\], this is \[Rep Name\] with \[Company\]. I know I’m catching you cold, so I’ll be quick.

The reason I’m calling is we’re speaking with small and midsize businesses that are looking at new ways to grow, improve customer acquisition, or make operations more efficient.

Is that something you’re focused on right now?”

---

# **Qualification Checklist**

The rep should ask about:

## **Business Need**

\[ \] What the prospect wants to improve.  
 \[ \] What problem they are trying to solve.  
 \[ \] What goal matters most right now.

Example questions:

“What are you mainly focused on improving right now?”

“What is one area of the business you would like to improve over the next few months?”

---

## **Current Process**

\[ \] How they handle the problem today.  
 \[ \] What tools, vendors, or internal processes they use.  
 \[ \] What is working.  
 \[ \] What is still frustrating.

Example questions:

“How are you handling that today?”

“What is working well?”

“What is still frustrating?”

---

## **Priority**

\[ \] Whether this is a current priority.  
 \[ \] Whether they are actively exploring solutions.  
 \[ \] What would make it worth a deeper conversation.

Example questions:

“Is this a current priority, or more of a future project?”

“What would make this worth exploring?”

---

## **Authority**

\[ \] Whether the prospect is the decision-maker.  
 \[ \] Whether anyone else needs to be involved.  
 \[ \] Who should attend the next call.

Example questions:

“Are you the person who usually evaluates new solutions like this?”

“Would anyone else need to be involved in the conversation?”

---

## **Readiness**

\[ \] Whether they are open to investing if there is fit.  
 \[ \] Whether they have looked at similar solutions before.  
 \[ \] Whether a meeting would be useful.

Example questions:

“Are you open to investing if the business case makes sense?”

“Have you tested anything similar before?”

---

# **Objection Handling Checklist**

## **If they say, “I’m busy”**

\[ \] Acknowledge it.  
 \[ \] Stay brief.  
 \[ \] Restate the reason for calling.  
 \[ \] Ask whether to send a short email or call another time.

Approved response:

“Totally understand. I’ll be quick. The only reason I called is to see if improving growth, customer acquisition, or operations is a focus for your business right now. Would it be better if I sent a short email, or should I try you another time?”

---

## **If they say, “Send me info”**

\[ \] Do not end the call immediately.  
 \[ \] Ask what would be most useful.  
 \[ \] Confirm whether they review this type of solution.  
 \[ \] Set a follow-up step.

Approved response:

“Happy to. So I send the right thing, what would be most useful for you to see? A quick overview, examples of how it works, or details on whether this is a fit for your business?”

---

## **If they say, “We already have something”**

\[ \] Acknowledge it.  
 \[ \] Do not criticize their current solution.  
 \[ \] Ask if they are happy with the current setup.  
 \[ \] Look for gaps or improvement areas.

Approved response:

“That makes sense. A lot of businesses we speak with already have something in place. Are you happy with how things are working today, or is there anything you still want to improve?”

---

## **If they ask about pricing**

\[ \] Do not guess.  
 \[ \] Do not make up a number.  
 \[ \] Say the sales team can explain pricing based on fit.  
 \[ \] Continue qualification if appropriate.

Approved response:

“Good question. I do not want to give you the wrong answer without understanding fit first. My role is to see if this is worth a conversation. The sales team can walk you through pricing and options based on your business.”

---

## **If they ask for a guarantee**

\[ \] Do not guarantee results.  
 \[ \] Be direct.  
 \[ \] Explain that fit needs to be evaluated.  
 \[ \] Redirect to the business problem.

Approved response:

“I want to be careful with that. We do not guarantee results. The right way to look at this is whether there is a real business problem, whether the solution fits, and whether it is worth exploring.”

---

## **If they say, “Not interested”**

\[ \] Do not argue.  
 \[ \] Ask one respectful clarification question if appropriate.  
 \[ \] Accept the answer if they are firm.  
 \[ \] Log the outcome correctly.

Approved response:

“Totally understand. Just so I do not follow up with something irrelevant, is this not a priority right now, or is it something you already have covered?”

---

# **Meeting Booking Checklist**

Book a meeting only if:

\[ \] The prospect has a real business goal or problem.  
 \[ \] The prospect is open to learning more.  
 \[ \] The prospect is the decision-maker or can involve the decision-maker.  
 \[ \] The prospect has some level of readiness.  
 \[ \] The meeting would be useful for the sales team.  
 \[ \] The rep has enough notes to support the handoff.

## **Approved Meeting Ask**

“Based on what you shared, this may be worth a quick conversation with our sales team.

The call is not a commitment. It is just to understand your business, your goals, and whether this could be a fit.

Would \[day/time\] work for a short intro call?”

---

# **Do Not Book Checklist**

Do not book the meeting if:

\[ \] The prospect has no interest.  
 \[ \] The prospect has no clear need.  
 \[ \] The prospect refuses to answer basic questions.  
 \[ \] The prospect only wants guaranteed results.  
 \[ \] The prospect has no ability to invest.  
 \[ \] The prospect is not the right person and will not share who is.  
 \[ \] The prospect asks not to be contacted again.  
 \[ \] The rep does not understand why the meeting would be useful.

---

# **Claim Control Checklist**

The rep must not say:

\[ \] Results are guaranteed.  
 \[ \] Revenue is guaranteed.  
 \[ \] Leads are guaranteed.  
 \[ \] The solution works for every business.  
 \[ \] Pricing is confirmed without approved pricing.  
 \[ \] Performance is guaranteed.  
 \[ \] The prospect must buy now.  
 \[ \] The product replaces everything they already use.  
 \[ \] The sales team will definitely solve their problem.

If the rep does not know the answer, say:

“I do not want to give you the wrong answer. The sales team can walk you through that.”

---

# **CRM Logging Checklist**

After every call, log:

\[ \] Company name.  
 \[ \] Contact name.  
 \[ \] Call date.  
 \[ \] Call outcome.  
 \[ \] Connected or no answer.  
 \[ \] What the business does.  
 \[ \] What the prospect said they care about.  
 \[ \] Current process or current solution.  
 \[ \] Interest level.  
 \[ \] Decision-maker status.  
 \[ \] Objections or questions.  
 \[ \] Meeting booked or not.  
 \[ \] Follow-up date.  
 \[ \] Next step.  
 \[ \] Lead status.

---

# **CRM Note Template**

“Spoke with \[Name\] at \[Company\]. They are currently focused on \[goal/problem\]. Today they handle this by \[current process/tool/vendor\]. \[Name\] is \[decision-maker status\]. Main question or objection was \[objection\]. I responded by \[summary\]. Next step is \[meeting/follow-up/disqualified\].”

---

# **Final Rep Self-Check**

Before marking the call complete, confirm:

\[ \] I opened the call clearly.  
 \[ \] I explained why I was calling.  
 \[ \] I asked qualification questions.  
 \[ \] I listened to the prospect’s answers.  
 \[ \] I did not make unsupported claims.  
 \[ \] I did not guess on pricing or details.  
 \[ \] I handled objections properly.  
 \[ \] I booked a meeting only if there was fit.  
 \[ \] I respected the prospect’s response.  
 \[ \] I logged the call in CRM.  
 \[ \] I set the correct next step.

# **Training: General SMB Cold Calling**

## **Training Goal**

Your job is to call small and midsize businesses, start a conversation, understand if there is a real need, and book a meeting with the sales team if the prospect is a fit.

You are not responsible for closing the deal. You are not responsible for giving pricing. You are not responsible for answering every detailed question.

Your job is simple:

Call the prospect.  
 Explain why you are calling.  
 Ask a few questions.  
 See if there is interest.  
 Book a meeting if it makes sense.  
 Log the call.

## **What Makes a Good Cold Call**

A good cold call is short, clear, and respectful.

You should not start with a long pitch. The prospect did not ask for the call, so get to the point quickly.

A good call should answer three things fast:

Who are you?  
 Why are you calling?  
 Why should they care?

## **Basic Cold Call Script**

“Hi \[First Name\], this is \[Rep Name\] with \[Company\]. I know I’m catching you cold, so I’ll be quick.

We’re speaking with small and midsize businesses that are looking for better ways to grow, improve customer acquisition, or make their operations more efficient.

Is that something you’re focused on right now?”

## **If They Ask, “What Is This About?”**

“Good question. I’m not calling to force a pitch. I’m just trying to understand if improving growth, customer acquisition, or operations is relevant for your business right now. If it is, I can set up a short conversation with our sales team.”

## **Questions to Ask**

Use a few of these questions. You do not need to ask all of them.

“What are you mainly focused on improving right now?”

“How are you handling that today?”

“What is working well?”

“What is still frustrating?”

“Is this a current priority, or more of a future project?”

“Are you the person who usually evaluates new solutions like this?”

“Would anyone else need to be involved?”

“Are you open to investing if the business case makes sense?”

## **When to Book a Meeting**

Book a meeting only if the prospect has some level of need, interest, authority, and fit.

A meeting is a good next step if:

They have a real problem or goal.

They are open to learning more.

They are the decision-maker or can bring the decision-maker.

They are willing to explore a solution if it makes sense.

## **Meeting Ask**

“Based on what you shared, this may be worth a quick conversation with our sales team.

The call is not a commitment. It is just to understand your business, your goals, and whether this could be a fit.

Would \[day/time\] work for a short intro call?”

## **Common Objections**

### **“I’m busy.”**

“Totally understand. I’ll be quick. Is improving growth, customer acquisition, or operations something your business is focused on right now, or should I try another time?”

### **“Send me info.”**

“Happy to. So I send the right thing, what would be most useful for you to see? A quick overview, examples of how it works, or whether this is a fit for your business?”

### **“We already have something.”**

“That makes sense. A lot of businesses already have something in place. Are you happy with how it is working today, or is there anything you still want to improve?”

### **“How much does it cost?”**

“Good question. I do not want to give you the wrong answer without understanding fit first. The sales team can walk you through pricing and options based on your business.”

### **“Can you guarantee results?”**

“I want to be careful with that. We do not guarantee results. The right way to look at this is whether there is a real business problem, whether the solution fits, and whether it is worth exploring.”

## **What Not to Do**

Do not guarantee results.

Do not make up pricing.

Do not over-explain.

Do not argue.

Do not keep pushing after someone clearly says no.

Do not book a meeting if there is no real fit.

Do not leave the call without logging notes.

## **CRM Note Template**

“Spoke with \[Name\] at \[Company\]. They are focused on \[goal/problem\]. They currently handle this by \[current process\]. \[Name\] is \[decision-maker status\]. Main question or objection was \[objection\]. Next step is \[meeting/follow-up/not interested\].”

## **Example CRM Note**

“Spoke with John at ABC Services. They are focused on improving follow-up and getting more consistent inquiries. They currently handle follow-up manually. John is the owner and decision-maker. He asked about pricing, and I explained that the sales team would walk through options after understanding fit. Meeting booked for Thursday at 2 PM.”
